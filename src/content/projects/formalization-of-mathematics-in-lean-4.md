## Overview

I am studying mathematical formalization in Lean 4 under the supervision of
Prof. Mykola Matviichuk at The Chinese University of Hong Kong. The work concerns
properties of skew-symmetric matrix graphs and how mathematical definitions and
arguments can be expressed in a form that a proof assistant can check.

This is ongoing supervised research. My focus is on understanding the mathematics
and examining the relationship between an informal argument and its formal
statement, rather than treating successful compilation alone as evidence that
the intended mathematical question has been addressed.

## Research Focus

Formalization requires making assumptions, definitions, and dependencies explicit.
A machine-checked proof establishes a statement within its encoded setting;
checking that this setting matches the intended mathematics remains an important
part of the work. I am interested in the points where an informal explanation
leaves something implicit that a formal development must specify.

## My Involvement

My involvement includes studying definitions and arguments, reviewing AI-generated
Lean proofs, and checking assumptions and formalized statements. I examine how
the informal reasoning is represented and work to understand the proof steps
rather than assuming that generated code captures the intended argument.

The distinction matters in both directions: an informal argument may omit a
necessary hypothesis, while a formal statement may encode a different claim
from the one a reader expects. Reviewing these possibilities is part of learning
to use formal verification carefully.
