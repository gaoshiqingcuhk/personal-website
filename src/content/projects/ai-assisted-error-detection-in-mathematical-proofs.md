## Overview

This independent exploratory project asks how AI-assisted reasoning might help
identify errors in mathematical proofs. I am interested not only in whether an
argument is judged correct or incorrect, but also in whether a critique identifies
the particular step, missing assumption, or unsupported inference that requires
attention.

A small preliminary pilot has been conducted. The broader research remains
incomplete, and the pilot does not establish reliable error-detection performance
on mathematical writing in general.

## Research Question

How can AI-assisted reasoning detect and localize errors in mathematical
arguments, and how can Lean 4 help verify selected findings? A useful answer
would need to distinguish a genuine mathematical objection from a plausible
but mistaken criticism. It would also need to distinguish an error in an
argument from a limitation of a model's understanding.

## Approach

The exploratory direction combines review of AI-generated critiques with
formal verification of selected claims where feasible. Lean 4 is a way to
check a precisely stated claim under explicit assumptions, not an automatic
validator of an entire informal document.

My focus is on examining the reasoning behind a proposed objection and its
location in the proof. Computational evaluation of proof-auditing approaches
is part of the broader research question; a defensible assessment would need
clear evaluation criteria and checks on both missed errors and false alarms.
These are evaluation requirements, not claims of completed validation.

## Current Status

The work is ongoing and exploratory. No numerical performance claims or
published research contribution are asserted here. Private pilot records, case
identifiers, and unpublished findings are excluded. A convincing model response
is a reason to investigate a step, not sufficient evidence that the proof is wrong.
